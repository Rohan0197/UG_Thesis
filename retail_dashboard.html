Model Architecture Decisions
Why each choice was made — for stakeholder transparency
LightGBM with MAE loss — Retail demand has extreme outliers (promotions, events). MAE loss is robust to these; MSE would overfit to spikes.
Walk-forward cross-validation — No look-ahead bias. Train on past, predict future, exactly as in production deployment.
Top 200 products only — Covers ~80%+ of revenue. Training on all 3,000+ products would be intractable and add noise from negligible items.
Quantile models (α=0.1, 0.9) — Three models trained: point forecast + 80% prediction interval bounds. This replaces the naive ±MAE band with statistically valid uncertainty.
lag_52 (annual seasonality) — Confirmed by SHAP. Walmart retail has strong yearly patterns. Without this, the model underestimates Q4 uplift.
SNAP days as a binary feature — Government food stamp payment days cause a measurable spike in FOODS demand. SHAP rank 3 confirms this signal.
price_vs_median_ratio — New feature: item price relative to its own 8-week median. Captures relative elasticity not captured by raw price_change.
Customer Intelligence Methodology
How CLV and churn scores are computed
RFM Segmentation — Quintile scoring on Recency, Frequency and Monetary value. 8 rule-based segments derived from score combinations.
BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma CLV — Probabilistic model fitted per customer. Predicts expected purchases and spend in next 12 months accounting for the probability the customer is already "dead" (churned).
Logistic Regression Churn — 5-fold cross-validated binary classifier on R, F, M scores. Outputs calibrated probability (0–1), not a heuristic score.
K-Means Clustering (k=4) — Validated with elbow method and silhouette score. 4 clusters correspond to High, Mid, Low value and Inactive customer tiers.
